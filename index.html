The Refund Math

Did the bigger tax refund cover what the last two years cost you?

Tuesday, October 06, 2026

The bigger refund vs. the bills that came with it

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act was sold on bigger tax refunds, and refunds did go up. The IRS says the average refund this spring was $3,276, up from $2,939 last year. That is $337 more.

Here is what other policy choices have cost the same family.

ItemPer householdSource
Bigger tax refund (OBBBA) +$337 IRS filing season statistics, average refund 2026 vs. 2025, through May 8.
Gas and diesel since the Iran war began −$945 Brown University Iran War Energy Cost Tracker.
Tariffs, 2025 and 2026 −$1,820 Tax Foundation: $1,000 in 2025 and $820 in 2026, after the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs.
Lost buying power from wages falling behind prices −$565 Real average weekly earnings fell 0.84% at an annual rate from February to August 2026 (BLS). Applied to average annual pay of $67,545 per worker.
Net for a typical family −$2,993
The refund covered about 1 in every 10 dollars of these costs.

And if you bought a house this year, it gets worse. In January 2025, Fannie Mae forecast mortgage rates would end 2026 near 6.3%. The Freddie Mac 30-year rate was 7.28% on October 1, 2026. That is a full percentage point higher. On a $330,000 loan (80% of a typical $410,000 home) the monthly payment is about $215 higher, or $2,584 a year. About 4 million households with adjustable-rate mortgages are paying the higher rates too, even though they aren't buying or refinancing.

Do your own math

A note on the mortgage line. Higher rates are partly the war and partly the deficit. The administration's answer, from Kevin Hassett and others, is that long-term rates rose because investors expect faster growth. If that were the main story, you would expect stronger real wages. The wage line above says otherwise.

Fine print. The periods don't all match. Gas covers the war so far. Tariffs cover 2025 and 2026. Wages are an annual rate. The refund is one filing season. Nothing is double-counted, but this is a rough household tally, not a single model. The wage figure is per worker, not per household.
