The Refund Math

Did the bigger tax refund cover what the last two years cost you?

Tuesday, October 06, 2026

The bigger refund vs. the bills that came with it

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act was sold on bigger tax refunds, and refunds did go up. The IRS paid out $324.8 billion in refunds this spring, up from $275.0 billion last year. Spread across the 99.1 million households that got a refund, that is about $500 more per household.

Here is what other policy choices have cost the same family.

ItemPer householdSource
Bigger tax refund (OBBBA) +$502 IRS filing season statistics through May 8: $49.8 billion more in refunds than 2025, divided by 99.1 million refunds.
Gas and diesel since the Iran war began −$945 Brown University Iran War Energy Cost Tracker.
Tariffs, 2025 and 2026 −$1,820 Tax Foundation: $1,000 in 2025 and $820 in 2026, after the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs.
Net for a typical family −$2,263
The refund covered less than 1 in every 5 dollars of these costs.

30-year mortgage rate vs. Fannie Mae's January 2025 forecast, weekly, Jan 2025 to Oct 2026

Source: Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey. Forecast line connects Fannie Mae's stated year-end points (about 6.5% at end-2025, 6.3% at end-2026).

And if you bought a house this year, it gets worse. In January 2025, Fannie Mae forecast mortgage rates would end 2026 near 6.3%. The Freddie Mac 30-year rate was 7.28% on October 1, 2026. That is a full percentage point higher. On a $330,000 loan (80% of a typical $410,000 home) the monthly payment is about $215 higher, or $2,583 a year. About 4 million households with adjustable-rate mortgages are paying the higher rates too, even though they aren't buying or refinancing.

Do your own math

A note on the mortgage line. Higher rates are partly the war and partly the deficit. The administration's answer, from Kevin Hassett and others, is that long-term rates rose because investors expect faster growth.

Fine print. The periods don't all match. Gas covers the war so far. Tariffs cover 2025 and 2026. The mortgage line is per year. The refund is one filing season. Nothing is double-counted, but this is a rough household tally, not a single model.
